Grammar rules

Gen Z texting often drops formal punctuation and capitalization on purpose. These patterns carry tone, so they are worth knowing.

Capitalization

  • All lowercase reads as relaxed and casual.
  • Full capitals (ALL CAPS) signal strong emotion or emphasis, and can read as yelling.
  • Capitalizing one word adds emphasis, for example "that was SO good".

Punctuation

  • A missing period can be neutral. A period at the end of a short message can feel cold or final.
  • Repeated punctuation such as "??" or "!!!" adds intensity.
  • Ellipses (...) suggest trailing off, hesitation, or unspoken meaning.

Letter stretching and shortening

  • Stretching a word, as in "sooo" or "yesss", adds enthusiasm.
  • Shortening, as in "rn" (right now), "tbh" (to be honest), or "idk" (I don't know), speeds up typing and keeps the tone casual.

Message structure

  • Breaking one thought into several short messages is common and feels conversational.
  • Replying with a single word, such as "bet" or "fr", is a complete response.

Slang glossary

bet
Okay, agreed, or sounds good. Also used to confirm a plan.
bussin
Really good, usually about food.
cap / no cap
A lie / not lying. "No cap" means "for real".
delulu
Short for delusional. Describes believing something unrealistic, often in a playful way.
fr / fr fr
For real. Used to emphasize agreement or sincerity.
giving
Used in "it's giving" to describe a vibe, as in "it's giving main character".
glow up
A noticeable improvement in someone's appearance, confidence, or life.
goated
The greatest of all time, or excellent.
highkey / lowkey
Openly or very much / secretly or a little.
ick
A sudden feeling of disgust or loss of attraction toward someone.
ate / ate that
Did something extremely well, or looked great.
mid
Average or mediocre. Can be an insult.
periodt
An emphatic way to end a statement, meaning "end of discussion".
rizz
Charm or the ability to attract someone romantically. "Rizzler" is a person who has it.
salty
Bitter, annoyed, or upset, often over something small.
simp
Someone who does too much for a person who does not return the effort.
sus
Short for suspicious. Something seems off.
tea
Gossip or the real story. "Spill the tea" means share the gossip.
understood the assignment
Did exactly what was needed, and did it well.
vibe check
Checking someone's mood or attitude. Can also be a joke about someone's energy.

Tone rules

  • Match the energy of the person you are texting. If they send short messages, keep yours short.
  • Use slang with people who use it too. Overusing it with others can feel forced.
  • Avoid all caps and heavy punctuation when you are upset. Say what you mean directly.
  • Read emoji in context. The same emoji can be sincere, joking, or sarcastic.
  • When a message could be read two ways, ask a clarifying question rather than guessing.
  • Keep jokes at your own expense or at shared expense. Avoid mocking people who are not part of the conversation.
  • Slang changes quickly. If a term is unfamiliar, ask instead of pretending to know it.
